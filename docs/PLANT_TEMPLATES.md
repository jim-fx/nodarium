# Plant Templates

The plant templates in `packages/runtime/templates` each model a real species. This file describes the structure of each plant and how its template builds it, so a template can be tuned against the real plant. Render a template with `pnpm render-graph <name>` in `app/`, or `nodarium render <name>` outside the repository.

Proportions are relative. The garden scales every plant to the same height, so only the ratios matter: leaf length to plant height, width to height, and so on.

## What the nodes can express

- A leaf is placed sideways from its path and faces up. `y_curve` bends it along its length, in radians: positive values bend it down (away from its face), negative values bend it up. `x_curve` curls the cross-section: positive gives a channel open to the top, negative rolls the margins under (revolute, or the Λ fold of a palm leaflet).
- The size of a leaf is `leaf.size * instance.size * (thickness of the path at that point + 0.01)`, times 100 for the length of `leaf` and `revolve` at size 1. `nodarium run` prints for every `instance` node how many instances it placed and how long and wide they are in meters, so check the size there instead of working it out. Leaves therefore shrink toward the tip of their path, unless `alpha -> curve -> size` on the `instance` node sets another profile. Such a curve has to make up for the taper of the path: its value at a point is the wanted size divided by the thickness there.
- `alpha` is where a branch or instance sits on its parent path, from 0 at the base to 1 at the tip. `alpha -> curve` into `length`, `angle` or `rotation` of a `branch` changes the branches along the parent, into `size` of an `instance` changes the leaves along the path. The hidden `angle` of an `instance` tilts it from sticking straight out toward the tip of its path (positive) or the base (negative), in radians, as it does for a `branch`. It is read for every instance, so a `random` or a curve of `alpha` gives every instance its own. A curve that drops to 0 hides the instances there, which leaves gaps between evenly spaced instances. Into `thickness` of a `stem` or `branch`, alpha goes along that path instead and the curve is its profile. `taper 0` turns off the linear taper, so the curve alone sets the profile. In `stem`, `gravity`, `rotate`, `noise` and `apply_force` alpha is where the path sits among all paths, from 0 at the first to 1 at the last. In `leaf` it is 0.
- `path_info` tells a node about the path it works on: its `position` among all paths, `index` (the number among the paths its node made on the same parent, it stays when other paths change), `count`, `depth`, `parent position` (where on its own parent it grows), `length`, `grown` and `age`, in this order the options 0 to 7 of `info` (a graph can name them as well, `"info": "parent position"`). A `branch` or `instance` works on the path it grows on, so a `path_info` into its `length` or `size` changes it from parent to parent, where `alpha` changes it along the parent. With `parent position` it reads the age of a frond or the height of a branch on the trunk. Scaling organs by the `length` of their parent changes little: a `random` draws from noise, so lengths gather around the middle of the range and the parents are all about as long.
- To let a plant grow with `time`, drive the hidden `growth` of `stem`, `branch` and `branch_from_points`, not their `length`: the `length` stays the full-grown length and `growth` (0 to 1) is the share of it that is there. Leaves, kinks and branches then stay where they are on the path and come one after another behind the tip. A `length` that shrinks with time moves everything on the path with it, the young plant is a small copy of the old one. `path_info` with `age` is how long ago the tip passed a spot, 0 at the tip: `age -> curve` into the `growth` of a branch or the `size` of an instance starts it when the tip reaches it. Let the `growth` of the stem rise above 1 (a curve with `max` 1.5) so the age keeps rising after the stem is full-grown and the last branches and leaves finish. A `length` that rises a little as well (0.7 to 1 of the full length) lets the nodes drift up while the path grows, like young internodes that stretch. See Growth in `PLANTARIUM.md`. The bamboo, default plant, fern, horsetail, knotweed, lady fern, lavender, maidenhair, palm (its fronds), sage and sapling grow this way. Where a flower sits on the tip of a stem that grows, as on the scapes of the daisy and the dandelion and the stems of the wild carrot, the stem still grows by its `length`, so the flower rides up with the tip: with `growth` it would be dormant until the stem is full-grown.
- `branch` spaces its branches evenly from `lowest_branch` to `highest_branch`, so `highest_branch 1` puts the last one at the tip of the path. `offset_single` moves every even branch (0, 2, 4, ...) up by that part of the distance to the next one: `offset_single 1` puts branch 2k at the height of 2k + 1, a pair per node.
- The `angle` of a branch is measured from its parent path, not from the world. Negative angles point back toward the base of the parent.
- `gravity` turns the tip of a path by `strength` × π/2 (a quarter turn) toward the ground: 1 an upright tip to horizontal, above 1 past it into a hook, like a nodding bud (`strength` 1.7 with `elasticity` 0.85 bends only the top). `strength` is read for every path, so `time` or a `path_info` with `grown` through a curve into it lets a bud nod and straighten up as it opens. A negative `strength` times the lean of a stem turns its tip back up, see the scapes of the dandelion. `branch` `angle` stays within ±1.5708: past a right angle it would be the same direction as a smaller angle turned around the path.
- `branch`, `gravity`, `noise` and `instance` only change the deepest paths by default. Instancing on a path _before_ branching it puts geometry only on that level, as in the grass, where the plumes sit on the flowering stems but not on the leaf blades.
- Paths taper linearly to zero unless `taper` is lowered. Every random value comes from the same seed.
- `random` draws from simplex noise, so its values gather around the middle of the range. To spread stems evenly around the plant, grow them with `branch_from_points` on the points of a `spiral`, at the golden angle, instead of turning them by a random angle.
- `rotate` turns every path around its own base. Its `angle` is read once per path, so a `random` gives every path its own angle, and `alpha` is where the path sits among all paths there, so a curve can sort them, for example by age. With `spread` on, path j of n is turned by `angle * j / n`: `rotate y` with `spread` and 6.28 spaces the stems of a `stem` node evenly around the plant.
- To fan the stems of a `stem` out in a plane, from `-a / 2` to about `a / 2`, two `rotate` nodes follow it and one `float` a feeds both: the first turns them by `a * -0.5` (a `math` multiply), the second by a with `spread`. Around x the stems stand like the fronds of a fan palm, after a `rotate x` of 1.5708 rad two `rotate y` spread the main veins of a palmate leaf for `leaf_surface`.
- `branch` turns every branch from the one before it by `rotation` around its parent, starting from the side (0 rad), 1.5708 rad points down and 4.7124 rad up. With one value branch i is turned by `i * rotation`. The rotation is read per branch and the turns add up, so an `alpha -> curve` into it can give every branch its own angle: the value at the alpha of branch i is the wanted angle of branch i + 1 minus that of branch i (plus 6.2832 where that is negative). Branch 0 always points to the side. The places only stay where the curve expects them with a fixed `amount`, see the lavender.
- `gravity` with a negative `strength` bends a path up instead of down, which makes shoots grow upward. It turns every segment by the same amount, so a path that is already upright tips over to the other side.
- Two `instance` nodes put leaves in opposite pairs: with `rotation 1.57`, the second one starts two steps lower, so its leaf k + 2 sits at the height of leaf k of the first and is turned 3.1416 rad from it. A curve on its size hides the two extra leaves at the bottom.
- A `branch` with an almost zero `length` is a point on its parent. An `instance` on it puts its instances in a ring around that point, a true whorl.
- `spiral` gives points around one spot that sit on no path, where `branch_from_points` grows a clump and `instance_from_points` places a rosette. A crown `stem` that nobody sees, only there for a `branch` or `instance` to grow on, isn't needed any more.
- A `shape` is one half of an outline in a box of 100 by 100: x from the margin (0) to the midrib (50), y from the tip (0) to the base (100), see Shapes in PLANTARIUM.md. `leaf` makes a slice across the leaf at every point, so an outline has to go down from y 100 to 0 without turning back, the shape editor sorts its points by y. Lobes that point back to the base, as on a dandelion, come from the veins of `leaf_surface`: side veins of `branch_from_points` with a negative `angle`. `mix_shape` blends two outlines by a factor, a `random` with `once` into it gives every seed its own.
- `revolve` turns a shape around its midrib into a closed body: a bud, a berry, a capsule, a receptacle. It is placed like a leaf (`instance` with `angle 1.5708` and `lowest_instance` and `highest_instance` 1 puts one on the tip of a path, along it), colored by `set_color` and bent by `deform`.
- `instance` turns its leaves up to the sky (`face` up). With `face` path a ring of bracts or petals faces the axis of its path, wherever the path points, so `y_curve` bends all of them alike around a head that nods. On an upright path both are the same.
- `cut` ends paths at a height in meters: grow the branches of a corymb longer than they need to be and cut them, all tips end on one flat top.
- Units: every angle is in radians, the `rotation` of `instance`, `branch`, `points_on_path` and `trunk_kink`, every `angle`, the `opening` of `umbel`, the `amount` of `deform`, the curls of `leaf` and the `angle` of `rotate`: 2.3998 is the golden angle (137.5°), 3.1416 half a turn (two ranks, opposite), 1.5708 a right angle (decussate pairs). Graphs from before version 14 took degrees for most of them and turns for `deform`, loading them converts them (see the migration note in [GRAPHS.md](./GRAPHS.md)). Lengths, thicknesses and heights are meters whatever the `unit` of the graph's meta says, the unit only changes what the editor shows.
- The range `nodarium nodes` prints after an input, like `[0..1]`, is the range of its slider in the editor, not a limit: a `curve` can have a negative `min`, a `gravity` a `strength` of 1.7. `check` only warns about a value far outside.
- `trunk_kink` bends paths at evenly spaced spots and gives the kinks as points, each pointing to the outer side of its bend. `rotation 3.1416` zigzags in one plane, and the first kink always bends to the side, so a horizontal branch zigzags in a horizontal plane. `instance_from_points` and `branch_from_points` put a leaf or a branch on every point, so a leaf sits on the outer corner of every bend, as on a zigzag stem. Points are positions, they don't follow a later `gravity` or `noise`: bend a level first, then kink it, then grow on it. `gravity` doesn't move what grows on a path either, so this is the order for every level anyway.

## Modelling a new plant

This section is for anyone, person or LLM, who builds a new template. It collects what worked for the templates below.

### Workflow

1. Write the species section first: the parts of the plant, how they are arranged, and numbers from a flora (counts, lengths, ratios). A flora gives ranges such as "20–35 pairs of pinnae" or "leaves 1–6 cm". Use them, not a picture from memory.
2. Go through the plant from the ground up and give every level of branching its own node: crown, main axes, side axes, then the organs on the last level. A `branch` adds a level of paths, an `instance` puts leaves or flowers on the deepest paths. Count the levels a flora names: once-pinnate is one `branch` for the fronds and one `instance` for the pinnae, 3-pinnate needs two more `branch` nodes.
3. Render the template with `pnpm render-graph <name> --out <dir>` and compare the front, side and top views with photos of the plant. Check the top view too: it shows whether the stems spread evenly and what outline the blade has. `pnpm nodarium render <name> --seeds 1,2,3,4` puts four seeds into one sheet at the same scale.
4. Render one unit up close: copy the graph, set `amount` of the first `branch` to 1 to get a single frond or shoot, and give it a fixed `angle` of 1.5708 with `noise` and `gravity` at 0 to see a flower head from above. The renderer frames the whole plant, so a small part only shows up when it is alone.
5. Change one thing at a time, render again, and write down what the nodes can't do in the template section ("missing: ...").

### Sizes

- Lengths of `stem` and `branch` are absolute. The thickness of a `branch` is relative to the thickness of its parent where it starts.
- An instance is about `100 * leaf.size * instance.size * (thickness + 0.01)` long, so it depends on the thickness of the path it sits on. Work that out before guessing a leaf size: the daisy leaves started ten times too large because the crown is thick. When a stem gets thinner, everything instanced on it shrinks with it. A point of `spiral` has scale 1, so a leaf of a rosette on it is about `101 * leaf.size * instance.size` long, whatever else the plant does.
- The thickness of a branch of `branch_from_points` is its `thickness` times the scale of the point, so on a `spiral` it is in meters.
- Organs on tapering paths get smaller toward the tip by themselves. For leaflets that stay the same size, set `taper` lower or put an `alpha -> curve` on the instance `size`.
- One unit is one meter, also in a graph with `"unit": "cm"`, so build the plant at its real size: a daisy 15 cm (0.15), a fern 90 cm, a palm 25 m. Take the heights from the flora. A plant under about a meter gets the unit `cm` in `projectTemplates`, so its lengths show in cm in the inputs.
- To resize a plant, scale every length (`stem` and `branch` `length`, the `thickness` of the first `stem`, the `origin`, the `random` and `curve` nodes that feed them, the `radius` and position of a `force`), divide the `scale` of every `noise` by the factor and multiply its `strength` by it. Then correct every `instance` `size` by `factor * (t + 0.01) / (factor * t + 0.01)`, with `t` the thickness of the path it sits on, as `+ 0.01` does not scale. Render the plant with `pnpm render-graph` before and after, the `leafArea` it prints has to change by the square of the factor.
- The garden scales every plant to the same height, so set ratios from the flora: frond to stipe, head diameter to scape length, leaf length to plant height.

### Patterns

- **Clumps:** there are three ways to grow many fronds or stems from one point.
  - `spiral` (`amount` n, `height` a few millimeters) → `branch_from_points`: the stems at the golden angle, without a crown. An `alpha -> curve` into the `angle` of `branch_from_points` sets them by age, low and spreading outside (alpha 0), upright in the middle (alpha 1), and a `math` add of a `random` ±0.1 rad breaks the regularity. The `thickness` is in meters. Use this when the age gradient matters, as in the fern. A crown that should show, a brown hub under the fronds, is a short `stem` of its own that nothing grows on.
  - A `stem` with `amount` n, a `rotate x` with a `random` angle that leans each one, and a `rotate y` with `spread` and 6.28 that spreads them around the plant. It has few nodes, but the lean is random.
  - The same `stem` and `rotate x`, with a `rotate y` whose angle is `path_info` index times 2.3998 (a `math` multiply): the stems turn by the golden angle, and an `alpha -> curve` into the angle of the `rotate x` leans them by their place, as on the points of a spiral. Use this for stems that are already a `stem`, like the scapes of a dandelion. With `origin` y a curve of alpha the stems start one above the other.
- **Rosettes:** a rosette whose leaves neither stack into a column nor fuse into one fan is a `spiral` (`amount` the number of leaves, `height` the rise from the oldest to the youngest leaf, about 1 cm) → `instance_from_points`. The golden angle spreads the leaves evenly around, the spiral keeps them at the ground. Its points sit on no path, so the size of a leaf is set by `size` alone, not by the thickness of a crown. A curve of `alpha` into the `angle` raises the young inner leaves (alpha 1) and lays the old outer ones flat, and one into the `size` makes the innermost smaller. A `random` with `per_instance` on the angle breaks the order. Instancing the leaves at the base of every scape instead puts them in as many columns as there are scapes, all leaves of a scape start from the same spot. The dandelion has its rosette on a spiral.
- **Basal leaves:** put the leaves at the very base of the stems (`lowest_instance 0`, `highest_instance` near 0). All stems start at the same point, so their leaves together form one rosette. The leaves stick out sideways from a leaning stem, so give them enough `angle` that the ones on the downhill side don't point into the ground. Otherwise the leaves stand in as many columns as there are stems and lean with them: put them on a `spiral` of their own instead, see Rosettes.
- **Compound leaves:** the rachis is a `branch`, every degree of division is one more `branch` with `rotation 3.1416` (two ranks, alternate), and the last segments are an `instance` with `rotation 3.14`. `lowest_branch` leaves the stipe bare. The outline of the blade is an `alpha -> curve` into the `length` of the pinna branch: lanceolate (short at the base, longest a third up, a long taper), deltate (longest at the base) or a strap (constant).
- **Composite heads that open:** give every scape one phase, `(time − start) × speed − path_info position × stagger`, and read it through a `curve` wherever the head changes: the `length` of the scape in the `stem`, its lean in `rotate`, the `strength` of its `gravity`, the `length` of the receptacle `branch` at its tip, and the `angle`, `size` and `index` of every `instance` on the receptacle. `path_info` position is the place of a scape among the scapes in `stem`, `rotate` and `gravity` and the place of its receptacle among the receptacles in `instance`, so all of them read the same value, which a `random` with `per_path` does not give on two different paths. The positions are evenly spaced, so a plant has every stage at once and the heads come one after another. An `instance` with `pick` index and a `join` of geometries swaps a part by the phase: a bud, the closed head and the bare receptacle, or a yellow ligule and a withered one. A size that a curve brings to 0 hides a part, like the florets inside the bud. All rings take `face` path, so they open alike on a scape that leans. See the dandelion.
- **Flat-topped clusters:** branches in two or three levels, each longer than it needs to be and curved up by a negative `gravity`, then a `cut` at the height of the top: every tip ends on the same plane, and an `instance` with `depth` as deep as the levels puts a head on each. Give the stems and the cut the same height, a `random` with `once`, so the clusters of all stems end at one top. See the yarrow.
- **Flower heads:** a `branch` of length 0.002 at the tip (`lowest_branch 1`, `angle 1.5708`, `taper 0`) is a point, and instances on it are rings. Stack several instance nodes on the same point for the parts of a flower: a disc, rays, bracts, a calyx, petals. A part that starts outside the centre gets a shape with no width for its inner part, like the daisy rays that start at the edge of the disc. Use `rotation 2.3998` (the golden angle) for many parts that should spread evenly, `2π / n` for an exact ring of n.
- **Domes and cups:** sectors of a circle (a wedge shape that ends wide) in a ring meet in the middle. A positive `angle` and `y_curve` bend them into a dome, `x_curve` rolls each one into a tube or bell.
- **Bent, rolled and twisted leaves:** a `deform` after the leaf bends it (also back above the middle, with a `curve` of `alpha` that changes sign), curls it into a channel or rolls its margin under (a `curve` of `alpha` across) or twists it. Chain curl and twist before bend, see Deform in PLANTARIUM.md.
- **Opposite and whorled leaves:** see the two `instance` nodes and the near-zero `branch` above.
- **Mixed organs on one spot:** `join` the geometries into the `geometry` of `instance` or `instance_from_points`, every instance picks one at random. `instance` can also favour the earlier or later ones (`bias`) or pick by `index`, for example `alpha` to change the leaves from the base to the tip. `leaf_surface` with `variants` gives several leaves of one vein graph as such a list (see Leaf variants in PLANTARIUM.md). The devil's backbone template predates this and still chains one `instance_from_points` per geometry, fed from one `random` through step curves into their `size`.
- **Vein leaves as a node group:** a leaf of `leaf_surface` takes a dozen nodes (midrib, side veins, margin, vein colour, deform), so put them into a group, as in the sapling ("Birch leaf"). The group has few inputs: `size`, `variants`, the blade colour and the vein colour, and puts out the list of leaves for `instance`. Inside it:
  - The midrib is a `stem` with `taper 0` and `amount` = `variants` (both come from the `variants` input), turned flat with `rotate x` 1.5708 rad. An `alpha -> curve` into its `thickness` is the profile of the blade around the midrib. A petiole is a thin first part of that curve, with the side veins starting above it. The petiole has to stay about half a cell of the leaf grid wide (`resolution.leaf` cells across the leaf) or the grid loses it, which leaves the blade floating off the twig. In the birch that is 0.4 of the thickest midrib at `resolution.leaf` 32 to 48.
  - The side veins are `points_on_path` (`rotation 3.1416`, `offset_single` for nearly opposite pairs) and `branch_from_points`. Their `length` is an `alpha -> curve` (the outline) times a `random` with _per path_, and their `angle` a `random` with _per path_. `points_on_path` and `branch_from_points` read these once per midrib, so every leaf variant gets its own width and vein angle. A `random_int` into `amount` gives every variant its own number of side veins.
  - The margin is `wave` × `alpha -> curve` (no teeth at the base) × a `random` with _per path_ for the depth of the teeth, into `margin`. `margin` is a share of the reach of the veins, so 0.2 is a birch-sized tooth on any leaf size.
  - The colour goes into `leaf_surface.color`: `mix_color(blade, veins, curve(alpha vein) × curve(path_info depth − 1))`, so the midrib (depth 1) is paler than the side veins. Don't put a `set_color` after `leaf_surface`, it would replace the vein colour. The blade colour is a group input, so the template can still mix it outside the group from a `random` with _once_ and `alpha` along the leaf. To get different shades on different leaves, a `path_info` _position_ into the factor of a `mix_color` gives every variant its own shade, because the veins of variant k come together in the list of paths. The instance then picks the shape and the shade at once.
  - `deform` chains bend the leaf at the end: curl → twist → bend. Positive curl makes a channel open to the top, negative bend tips the leaf down.
  - The leaf grid is set by the `resolution.leaf` setting of the template, not on the node. Triangles grow with it and with the number of teeth: a birch leaf has about 230–290 triangles at 40 on average, against 144 for the `leaf` it replaces. Variants cost a few milliseconds each, placing them costs almost nothing.
- **Colours:** a `set_color` after the nodes that make and shape a level of paths colours that level (its hidden `depth` is 1, the deepest), a `set_color` on a `leaf` colours the leaf. A `mix_color` with a curve of `alpha` into its factor makes a gradient from base to tip, `alpha` set to _across_ from the midrib to the margin, so a leaf gets a pale margin or spots. A `random` with _once for the graph_ into the factor gives every seed its own shade, one with _once per path_ every path. For leaves that differ from each other, put several `set_color` nodes on the same leaf into a `join` and let the `instance` pick: at random, or by `index` with `alpha` (old leaves at the base of a shoot, young ones at its tip) or `path_info` _parent position_ (the age of a frond). Colours look as their value in the viewer: leaf greens around sRGB 60–100, 90–135, 40–60 read natural, brighter ones look like plastic.
- **Variation:** organs that are regular in nature (rays, pinnae, petals) get little random variation, around ±10% of their size. Stems, fronds and branches get more, in length and angle. A count that a flora gives as a range, like 2–5 leaves per branchlet, is a `random_int` into the `amount` of a `branch` or `instance`: it draws a new count for every parent path, so every frond or flower head gets its own. One seed drives all random values, so check a few seeds with `--seed`.

### Realism

- Counts read as more realistic than exact sizes: 40 rays instead of 12, 20 pairs of pinnae instead of 8. Take them from the flora.
- Real plants are rarely symmetric or straight. A little `noise` on every axis and a tilt by age make a plant look grown instead of built.
- Gravity shows the stiffness of a part: rigid parts (agave leaves, culms) get high elasticity or none, soft ones (fern fronds, grass blades) arch or hang.
- Leave out what the nodes can't show well rather than faking it, and say so in the template section.
- Describe a part in words before modelling it, from a botanical drawing or a flora: the ratio of length to width, where it is widest, how the base narrows into the stalk, the tip, the veins, the cross-section, how it is held. Then build it to that description and compare again. A leaf modelled from a vague idea looks generic, see the plantain.
- **Leaves arch.** A blade that sticks out straight looks stiff. A `deform` bend that grows toward the tip (a `curve` of `alpha` times a `random` per leaf) lets the leaf rise in the direction of its stalk and then tip over under its weight, the longer leaves more. The soft parts of a plant all react to gravity.
- **No two leaves alike.** Vary the angle, the bend, the twist and the outline of every leaf: a `random` per leaf on the angle and twist, and a `join` of three or four outlines with their own deforms. A rosette of identical leaves at regular angles looks built.
- **Parts are attached.** Every leaf, root and branch visibly grows out of its parent: a leaf base stays wide enough to see where it meets the crown, a petiole doesn't thin to a needle, and a leaf that starts in the air next to the stem is a bug. Where a branch leaves a stem, the thickness of both matches, so the joint is smooth.
- **No separate pieces at junctions.** A crown or a receptacle that shows as a disc or a plug between roots and stems looks wrong. Keep the crown small and hidden under the leaves, and give it the colour of what grows from it.
- **Roots** spread all around the crown (`rotate y` with `spread`), bend and wander (`noise`, `gravity`) and differ in length. Straight, even roots or roots to one side only look artificial.
- **Thin organs are paths, not leaves.** Stamens, filaments, hairs and pedicels are a `branch` with a tiny geometry (a `revolve`) at its tip, from an `instance` with `lowest_instance 1`. A flat leaf in their place looks like a petal.
- **No intersections in heads.** Petals, florets and buds of a head that cross each other are the first thing a viewer sees. Make the organs narrow enough for their count, give the rings `face` path and set their angles so neighbouring rings don't overlap, open and closed.
- Keep an eye on the size of the result: the instance count multiplies with every level. The 3-pinnate maidenhair has about 12,000 instances. Lower `resolution.curve` and `resolution.circle` in the template settings for finely divided plants.

### Growth and variation

Every template grows over the `time.t` setting and gives a different plant for every seed. The template sections below describe the full-grown plant and give the middle of a range where a count or a size is random.

- **Growth:** stems, scapes and roots grow by the `growth` input of `stem` and `branch`, not by a growing `length`: with `growth` the path keeps its full length and shape and extends from its base, while a growing length stretches the whole path, which looks like it scales. A `time` node goes through a `curve` into `growth`. Stems that should not grow in step subtract a `random` with `once per path` from the time, so every stem starts at its own time and all are done at 1. Thickness and the strength of `noise` follow with curves of their own, so a young stem is not a scaled-down old one.
- **Everything grows.** Roots too, and first: the roots grow before the leaves, the leaves before the flowering stems. Stagger the parts within a level widely, a few leaves at a time rather than all at once (see the daisy and the plantain).
- **Compact organs at a tip appear whole.** A flower head, a spike or a bud that grows by `growth` builds up from its base, part by part, which looks wrong. Give its `branch` `growth 1`, so it appears as a whole once its stem reaches it, and let it swell from a small bud: a curve of the same phase into its `length` and into the `size` of its instances.
- **Nothing jiggles.** A part that moves around while the plant grows (a `noise` read along a path whose length changes, a position that depends on a growing count) looks broken in the animation. Render a sheet over `--times` and check that every part only grows, opens and bends, never jumps.
- **Order along a path:** subtract a `curve` of `alpha` (or of `path_info` with `parent position`) from the time, so branches and leaves come from the base to the tip. Multiply the time first by 1 plus the largest delay, so everything is full-grown at 1.
- **Leaves at their own time:** `time × (1 + spread) − random(0–spread, once per instance)` into the growth curve of the instance size. Other inputs of the instance node that read the same value agree with it, like an angle that opens as the leaf grows. A random that starts below 0 gives some leaves a head start.
- **Per seed:** a `random_int` on the amounts, and a `random` with `once for the graph` on the `max` of a growth curve or of another random, for the height, the leaf size or the lean of the whole plant. `uniform` spreads plants evenly over the range, without it most of them look alike. Seeds differ clearly when one such random drives several traits together through curves of its own: in the dandelion one `habit` from 0 (a small lawn plant: short flat leaves, many short scapes that lean out, small heads) to 1 (a plant of the meadow: long ascending leaves that arch over, few tall scapes) sets the length, angle and arch of the leaves, the number, length, lean and thickness of the scapes, the size of the heads and the taproot. A second one sets how deep the leaves are lobed, two more how far the plant is in its flowering. Traits drawn one by one average out, the plants look alike.
- **Small instances are dropped.** An instance scaled below about 1e-4 counts as collapsed. A leaf of `size 1` under an instance size of 0.01 is close to that and vanishes while it grows. Keep the leaf small and the instance size near 1.

An `alpha -> curve` sets how far they rise: the lowest lie 0.2 rad above the ground and are the longest (16 cm), the ones in the middle of the crown stand at 1.05 rad and are about half as long, so the shoots start all over a low frame instead of at one point.

Every woody stem has five places for a shoot, from 30 % of its length to its tip. The four upper ones are flower stems. An `alpha -> curve` into the branch `rotation` turns each of them up around the woody stem, a little to alternating sides (the turns add up from branch to branch, so the curve holds the differences). Their `angle` is the angle of their woody stem above the ground, which `path_info` parent position gives through the same curve, plus how far they lean out: 0.5–0.95 rad on the lowest woody stems, 0.17 rad in the middle. So every flower stem stands up whatever its woody stem does, the outer ones lean out and the inner ones are upright, and the spikes end on a dome. Before, the flower stems went on in the direction of their woody stems and splayed almost flat. A weak negative gravity lets them arch up a little. The first place is the branch that always points to the side: a short leafy shoot without flowers that turns up, the skirt of the mound. A `random` through a step curve into the `growth` decides which places grow a shoot at all, 55–95 % of them by seed.

The lower 40 % of a flower stem carries 14–24 linear leaves at 1.5708 rad steps (`rotation 1.57`, decussate), a side shoot carries them almost to its tip. The leaves are revolute (`x_curve -0.6`) and ascending along the shoot (`angle 0.6981`). The top 8–18 % carries 110 tubular florets about 1 cm long, angled up along the stem (`angle 0.7854`), so they press together into a spike 2–10 cm long and about 1 cm thick. Their size follows a curve over the spike that drops to 0 twice: two separate whorls sit below the dense part. The middle stays bare as the peduncle. The whorls are bands of florets in a spiral, not true rings.

**Per seed:** one `habit` from an upright plant with long flower stems to a low, wide cushion with short ones sets the length of the flower stems (1 to 1.5 times 30–36 cm) and how far the outer ones lean out. Apart from it, the size of the whole plant, the number of woody stems, how many places grow a shoot, the length of the spike and the hues of leaves and florets differ.

**Colours:** The crown and the woody stems are grey-brown, the flower stems grey-green. The leaves are grey-green, one shade per seed. The florets are lavender with a dusky calyx at their base, one hue per seed between lavender blue and purple.

## Maidenhair Fern: _Adiantum raddianum_ (delta maidenhair)

Family Pteridaceae.

**Parts:** short creeping rhizome, fronds with a long, dark, polished stipe, a 3–4-pinnate blade and stalked, fan-shaped ultimate segments. The sori sit on their outer margins under folded-back flaps.

**Template:** a `stem` node grows 10–25 thin fronds, 21–49 cm long. The segment shape has a stalk and a wedge that widens into a broad, lobed margin, its size is one value per seed. Light gravity on the pinnae and pinnules makes the blade droop. A young frond is a crozier: the strength of its gravity starts at 7.5 and falls as it grows, so it unrolls. Lower resolutions keep the 12,000 segments fast. The fronds lean at random instead of by age, so young upright fronds are not always in the middle. The fourth division and the sori are missing.

## Sage: _Salvia officinalis_ (common sage, garden sage)

Family Lamiaceae.

The upper surface is wrinkled (_rugose_), the lower one white with hairs. The petiole is up to 3 cm. The leaves keep about the same size along the shoot, get smaller toward its tip, and often carry tufts of small leaves in their axils. The calyx is 8–14 mm, often purplish. The corolla is 15–30 mm, with a tube that is mostly inside the calyx, a straight to slightly sickle-shaped hooded upper lip (_galea_) and a broad, three-lobed lower lip that bends down.

**Template:** 9 woody stems grow from the points of a `spiral` at the golden angle, spread evenly around the plant, and thicken with time. They start 0.2–1.05 rad above the ground, so the low ones lie outward and the steep ones fill the middle of the mound. A negative gravity bends them up and noise makes them gnarled. Each carries 7 shoots from 18% up, to both sides of the stem in turn (`rotation 3.1416`), and a negative gravity of 0.45–0.75 turns every shoot up, so none hangs below the stem. Every shoot has 8 opposite, decussate leaf pairs (two `instance` nodes, see above) and 8 smaller pairs turned 1.5708 rad from them as the axillary tufts. The leaf shape has a petiole a third as long as the blade, an oblong blade 2.4 times as long as wide, and a crenate margin. The leaves are large for the flowers and almost flat, so the plant reads as a mound of leaves. Only some shoots flower, a tenth to four tenths of them by seed: a `random` once per path plus a `random` once for the graph goes through a step `curve` into the `growth` of the `branch` at the tip of the shoot (`angle 1.5708`), so the other shoots never grow their flowering stem. A flowering stem has 3 pairs of small, almost sessile leaves and 5 verticillasters, well apart, in its upper 60%. Every verticillaster is a branch of length 0.002 with 6 flowers in a ring. A flower is three instances on the same point: a calyx rolled into a toothed bell (`x_curve 2.2`), the upper lip (`x_curve -1.6`, a hood) and the lower lip, which has no width inside the calyx and bends down past its mouth (`y_curve 1.5`). Random sizes make some flowers buds. Bracts, the square stems and the hairs are missing.

**Colours:** The woody stems are brown, the shoots grey-green turning woody at the base. The leaves are felty grey-green, one shade per seed, the axillary tufts lighter. The flowering stems are purplish. Every flower has a purplish calyx and violet-blue lips, one hue per seed, the lower lip lighter with a pale throat.

## Wild Carrot: _Daucus carota_ (Queen Anne's lace)

Family Apiaceae.

**Parts:** taproot, a basal rosette, a branched, finely hairy flowering stem with alternate stem leaves, and compound umbels: rays that carry umbellets, whose pedicels carry the small white florets. Bracts sit under the umbel.

**Structure:** A biennial. In the first year it forms a rosette, in the second it grows a flowering stem 0.6–1.2 m tall that branches in its upper part. The leaves are 2–3-pinnate and feathery, with narrow, pointed last segments. The basal leaves are the largest, and the stem leaves get smaller toward the top. The main stem ends in the largest umbel, and every side branch ends in a smaller one. An umbel is 6–15 cm across with 20–50 rays. The outer rays are the longest and curve upward, so the top is flat to slightly domed. Each umbellet has 15–60 florets. Often a single dark purple floret sits in the centre. After flowering, the umbel curls inward into a "bird's nest".

**Template:** a main `stem` 0.8–1 m long with a little `noise`. 3 side branches grow from its upper half (`rotation 2.3998`). An `alpha -> curve` makes the lower ones longer, and a negative `gravity` bends them up. A second `stem` node grows 3 slender stalks, 45–75 cm, from the base:

- A `rotate x` leans them 0.3–0.59 rad at random, and a `rotate y` with `spread` fans them around the plant.
- A negative `gravity` curves them back up, so they stand like a vase.

A `join` combines the main stem and the stalks.

Every tip of the stem, the side branches and the stalks gets an umbel from an `instance` (`angle 1.5708`, `depth 2`). Its `geometry` is a `join` of two baked umbels, an open one and a closed bud, and it uses `pick` index:

- The `index` is `path_info` length through a step curve, times a `random`.
- The main stem is the longest stalk, so its terminal umbel is always open. The shorter stalks get an open umbel or a bud at random.
- The `size` comes from `path_info` depth, so umbels on the main stem and the basal stalks are larger than on the side branches.

The open umbel is built at the origin along -z and baked:

- An `umbel` node (24 rays, `depth 2`, `opening 1.4835`, `umbellet_scale 0.17`) sits on a 2 cm stub.
- A point `force` 10 cm out on the axis, used by `apply_force` with `depth 2`, pulls the outer rays up into an arc while the inner ones barely change. The top stays flat.
- An `instance` puts a ring of five petals at the end of every bent pedicel. The umbel's `points` output is computed before the bend, so it isn't used here.
- The involucre is a `branch` of 10 bracts, 4.5 cm long, off the top of the stub, each with 6 thread-like lobes from a second `branch`. A point `force` behind the umbel bends them so they spread and droop.
- A `join` adds the involucre to the umbel before `to_geometry`.

The bud is a second `umbel`: 16 rays, 1.8 cm, `opening 0.384` and `shape 1`, so the rays stand close together. Its bracts point up toward the tip (`angle 0.8727`).

The leaves are grown live, in a chain of their own that a `join` adds to the stalks before the output. The `gravity` and `noise` of the leaves only work on the deepest paths, so they would bend the stalks too if the leaves grew on the same list. The joined lists contain the stem twice, and the second copy gets new ids.

- The petioles:
  - The rosette is 9–12 petioles off a 1 cm crown at the base, each 16–26 cm long and rising 0.26–0.87 rad.
  - The stem leaves are 5 petioles on the main stem from 14% to 78%. An `alpha -> curve` times a `random` makes them shorter toward the top.
- A `join` combines both kinds of petioles. A `gravity` of random strength (0.15–0.45) per petiole and a gentle `noise` then arch each leaf by its own amount.
- 12–16 pinnae grow on the outer two thirds of the petiole, in opposite pairs (`rotation 3.1416`, `offset_single 1`). Their length is `path_info` length of the petiole times an `alpha -> curve`, so they get shorter toward the tip, and every leaf keeps its proportions at any size.
- Every pinna is covered by one flat, deeply lobed blade:
  - an `instance` at the base of the pinna with `angle 1.5708`, so the blade lies along the pinna
  - its `size` is `path_info` length of the pinna times 94, so the blade is as long as the pinna

The petals are tiny, so their `leaf.size` is small and their `instance.size` is large. Before #480 an instance that scaled below about 1e-4 was dropped as collapsed, now only one smaller than a thousandth of the largest of its node is, so the split no longer matters.

**Colours:** The stems are green, a little browner at the base, the leaves green. The rays of the open umbel are pale green, the florets white and the bracts green. The bud has pinkish florets. A single dark purple floret sits in the middle of the open umbel on most plants (a `random` once for the graph): an `instance` on the stub puts a leaf there whose bare stalk reaches the florets and whose round end a curve of `alpha` into `y_curve` turns to face along the axis.

Missing: the hairs, the bird's nest of the fruiting umbel, and the sheathing leaf bases. The stem leaves don't sit in the axils of the branches, and the lobes of a pinna are not divided again as in the finest leaves.

## Dandelion: _Taraxacum officinale_ (common dandelion)

Family Asteraceae.

**Parts:** taproot, a basal rosette of leaves, hollow leafless scapes with milky sap, and a flower head (capitulum) on each: an involucre of green bracts, the outer ones bent back, and 100–200 yellow ligulate florets. The head closes again after flowering and becomes the round seed head ("clock"): every achene carries its pappus, a parachute of fine bristles, on a long thin beak.

**Structure:** A perennial rosette 5–40 cm across. The leaves are 5–30 cm long, oblanceolate, runcinate: deeply cut into triangular lobes that point back toward the base, with a larger triangular end lobe, often a reddish midrib. In a lawn they lie flat, in tall grass they stand up. The scapes are 5–30 (–40) cm, ascending and curved up, one head each, 2–5 cm across. The bud is a green egg with the outer bracts already bent back. In the open head the ligules spread in several rows from a cup of upright inner bracts, the inner ones shorter and more upright. After flowering the head closes into a narrow cone with the withered ligules as a tuft on top, and the scape often lies down while the seeds ripen. Then it stands up, grows on and the head opens into the clock, 3.5–5 cm across, the inner bracts bent down. When the seeds are gone a pale bare receptacle is left. A plant in spring has buds, open heads, closed heads and clocks at the same time.

**Template:** a taproot (`stem` turned down and bent by `noise`, with 2–5 side roots from a `branch`), a rosette and the scapes, joined at the end. Nothing grows on a crown stem: the rosette sits on a `spiral`, the scapes are a `stem` of their own.

The rosette is a `spiral` of 8–18 points at the golden angle, 8 mm high, and an `instance_from_points` that puts a leaf on each. A curve of `alpha` and a `random` per leaf raise the inner leaves by up to 0.35 rad over the outer ones, and the innermost are the shortest. A leaf is a `leaf_surface` with 8 variants: a midrib whose `alpha -> curve` thickness makes a short winged petiole, a wing along the midrib and a broad triangular end lobe, and 8–14 side veins in nearly opposite pairs (`offset_single 0.85`) at an `angle` of −0.3 to −1 rad, so the lobes point back to the crown. A `gravity` on the midrib arches every variant by its own amount, and the blade follows it. A `noise` in y on the side veins lifts and lowers the lobes, a `wave` of 40 teeth into `margin` makes the small teeth, and a `deform` curl of 0.45 folds the blade into a shallow channel, so a leaf seen from its edge is not a line.

The scapes are a `stem` of 3–10, leaned by a `rotate x`, turned by the golden angle from one to the next (`rotate y` with `path_info` index × 2.3998 plus one turn per seed), and bent back up by a `gravity` whose strength is −0.65 times the lean of the scape, so a scape leaves the rosette at an angle and its head faces the sky. Every scape has a phase, `(time − 0.28) × season − path_info position × stagger`: 0 is a bud in the middle of the rosette, 1 a bare receptacle. Curves of the phase drive the scape and every part of its head:

| phase        | the head                     | the scape                              |
| ------------ | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| 0 to 0.24    | a bud that swells            | grows to half its length               |
| 0.24 to 0.3  | opens                        |                                        |
| 0.3 to 0.5   | in flower                    | 0.5 to 0.7 of its length               |
| 0.5 to 0.68  | closed, the ligules withered | leans 0.35 rad further out and hangs   |
| 0.68 to 0.76 | opens into the clock         | stands up and grows to its full length |
| 0.86 to 1    | the seeds fly off one by one |                                        |

The head sits on a `branch` at the tip of the scape, the receptacle with the tubes of the florets: 1 cm long, as high as the involucre, until it shrinks to 2.6 mm under the clock. Five `instance` nodes work on it, all with `face` path:

- 13 outer bracts at its base, spreading on the youngest bud (0.3 rad) and bent back from then on (−0.9 to −1.15 rad).
- 16 inner bracts, lanceolate and curled around the head (`x_curve` 0.9): pressed to the bud (1.42 rad), a cup under the open head (1.2 rad), closed again (1.45 rad) and bent down under the clock (−1 rad).
- One body, picked by the phase from a `join` of three `revolve` shapes (`pick` index): the egg of the bud, which shrinks into the cup while the head is open, the narrow cone of the closed head, and the pale dome of the receptacle.
- 130–165 ligules on the top sixth of the receptacle, above the tips of the inner bracts. A curve of `alpha` spreads the lowest flat (−0.08 rad) and stands the ones at the top up (1.38 rad), and makes those half as long, so the head is a dome. In the bud and in the closed head all of them stand up (1.47 rad) and are short. The geometry is picked by the phase: a yellow strap with a toothed tip, or the same strap brown and rolled up, the tuft on the closed head.
- 110 seeds. A seed is baked from paths by `to_geometry`: a brown achene (a `stem` of 3.5 mm), its beak (a `branch` of 12.5 mm at its tip) and a pappus of 12 bristles (an `umbel` with `opening` 0.95). A curve of `alpha` along the receptacle tilts them from −0.85 rad at its base to 1.57 rad at its top, the arcsine of an even step, so with the golden angle around they stand evenly on a sphere. While the clock opens they swing out from 1.47 rad, a brush that unfolds. `phase + random per instance 0–0.14` through a curve that drops to 0 at 1 takes every seed off at its own time.

`path_info` position is the place of a scape among the scapes in the `stem`, `rotate` and `gravity` and the place of its receptacle among the receptacles in the `instance` nodes, so the scape and its head read the same phase. The scape grows by its `length`, so the head rides up on its tip.

**Per seed:** one `random` with `once` is the habit, from a lawn plant (leaves of 6–12 cm that lie 0.14 rad above the ground, 6–10 scapes of 6–9 cm that lean far out, heads 3 cm across) to a meadow plant (leaves of 13–26 cm that rise at 0.65 rad and arch over, 3–7 scapes of 21–31 cm, heads 4.5 cm across, a longer and thicker taproot). A second one sets the lobes, from long lobes with deep sinuses to a shallowly toothed, wavy leaf (the length of the side veins and the `smooth` of the `leaf_surface`). `season` (0.85–1.3) and `stagger` (0.5–0.8) say how far the oldest scape is and how far the youngest is behind it: one plant is in full flower with its first clock, the next has lost half of its seeds. The number of leaves, their size, the shade of green and the side the scapes turn to are drawn once per seed too. The template has `seed` 1 in its settings, a lawn plant with every stage on it, so the app opens the plant that `nodarium render dandelion` draws. Seed 2 is a plant between the two habits and seed 3 a meadow plant early in its flowering.

**Growth:** the taproot first, then the leaves one after another from the outside in, each by `curve(time × 2 − 0.9 × alpha − random per leaf 0–0.4)` into its size. A scape is not there until its phase is near 0 (a curve of the phase plus 0.12 into its length, its thickness and the sizes of its bud): the first bud shows in the middle of the rosette at about time 0.17, the others follow by their place. The template stops in the middle of the flowering, so at time 1 every stage is on the plant.

**Colours:** leaves from dark to yellowish green, one shade per seed, lighter toward the tip, with a pale midrib that is reddish at the base. The scapes are pale green and purplish at the base, the taproot brown. The bracts are green with darker tips, the ligules golden yellow and more orange at their base, the closed head green with a brown tuft, the pappus white on brown achenes and a pale receptacle.

Missing: the hairs, the hollow of the scape, the milky sap. The bud and the closed head are smooth bodies with the inner bracts hidden in them, not bracts that overlap. The ligules are flat straps without the stamen tube and the forked style. The scapes come up from one spot and all at their own angle, they don't push through between the leaves. Old leaves don't yellow.

## Yarrow: _Achillea millefolium_ (common yarrow)

Family Asteraceae.

**Parts:** creeping rhizome, upright flowering stems, alternate feathery leaves, and a flat-topped compound corymb of many small flower heads, each with about five white ray florets around a cream disc.

**Structure:** A perennial 20–80 cm tall that grows in clumps. The stems are upright, little branched below the inflorescence. The leaves are 5–15 cm long and narrow, 2–3 times pinnately divided into fine segments ("millefolium"), the lower ones the largest, the upper ones smaller and sessile. The inflorescence is a dense, flat-topped corymb 5–15 cm across. A head is 4–6 mm across, with usually 5 short, broad white (sometimes pink) rays and 10–20 cream disc florets.

**Template:** 1–3 stems 32–88 cm tall, leaning up to 0.4 rad, turned around the plant at random and grown by `time`. A leaf is made of paths: 4 rachis variants (`stem`, arched by `gravity`) with 20–27 pinnae in two ranks and 6–9 segments on each pinna, longest in the middle of the leaf, baked by `to_geometry` into four feathery geometries. 8–13 of them lie in a basal rosette on the points of a `spiral`, 6–9 large ones sit on the lower half of each stem and 11–17 smaller ones, more upright, on the upper half.

One `random` with `once` sets the corymb: it makes the heads 0.7–1.4 times as large and the side clusters fewer, so a plant has one or two big flat tops or up to five smaller ones. The side clusters are a `branch` of 0–5 from 80–96% of the stem, 0.75–1.1 rad from it, curved up by a negative `gravity`, as long as the stem above them so their tips come near its top. An `umbel` with a flat top (`shape` 0, 2 layers of 4–6 rays) ends the stem and every side branch, and an `instance` puts a head on every ray. The clusters open from the lowest to the top one, their rays growing with the heads. The head is chosen by `time` from two baked geometries: a green bud cluster, or the open cluster of 5–7 rays and umbellets with a floret of six white rays and four yellow disc lobes on each tip, and a rim of green buds below.

**Colours:** stems brownish at the base and green above, the leaves from dark to yellow-green per plant, the rays white, on some plants tinged pink, the disc yellow, the buds pale green.

Missing: the rhizome, the hairs, spent brown heads. A plant has 0.5–1.1 million triangles, most of them in the heads.

## Great Mullein: _Verbascum thapsus_ (great mullein, common mullein)

Family Scrophulariaceae.

**Parts:** taproot, a basal rosette of large leaves in the first year, in the second a single flowering stem with alternate clasping leaves whose bases run down the stem as wings, and a dense terminal spike of buds, bracts and yellow flowers. Every part is covered in grey woolly hairs.

**Structure:** A biennial 1–2 m tall. The rosette is 30–60 cm across, its leaves oblong, 15–45 cm, soft, grey-green, with a wavy margin, the inner ones more upright. The stem is stout and unbranched, its leaves get smaller toward the top and stand up more. The spike is 20–50 cm long and about 3–5 cm thick, packed with buds; only a band of it flowers at a time. The flowers are 1.5–3 cm across, five broad rounded yellow petals, almost flat.

**Template:** a crown `stem` 4.5 cm long carries the rosette as one spiral: 26–42 leaves at the golden angle from its base to its tip. A curve of `alpha` lays the outer ones almost flat (0.26 rad) and stands the inner ones up (1.2 rad), a second one makes them longest a little inside the rim (up to 40–55 cm, by a `random` with `once` that scales all leaves of a plant) and less than half of that in the middle, and a `random` per leaf varies both. So the rosette is a mound 60–90 cm across, not a flat disc. A leaf is a node group around a `leaf_surface` with 5 variants and a wavy margin, grey-green with pale veins. The rosette bends its leaves less than the stem does (a `deform` of its own), so the outer ones lie on the ground instead of in it, and the crown has no `noise`: on a path this short it tilts the leaves every way.

The stem is 0.9–2.1 m tall, leans up to 0.1 rad and grows by `time` after the rosette. 16–26 leaves sit on its lower part, from just above the rosette, and 12–20 smaller ones above them, each at 42° ± 22° and 60° ± 18° from the stem and a `random` size. The lowest are a little shorter than the rosette leaves and they shrink steadily to the spike, so the plant is a cone of leaves from the rosette up. A `deform` bends every one of them by its own amount (−0.35 to 0.12 rad). Two `instance` nodes put a decurrent wing under each of them: a triangular `leaf` along the stem (−1.5 rad), curled around it by a `deform`, short under the lowest leaves so it stays above the ground.

The spike is a `branch` of 200–260 short clusters in pairs (`offset_single 0.5`) from 42–66 % of the height of the stem to its tip, 62° from it, their lengths an `alpha -> curve` times a `random` per path, bent apart by a fine `noise`. Every cluster carries 22–38 buds and 2 bracts. The flowers bloom in a band that climbs the spike: `path_info` parent position minus 1.92 × `time` into a curve puts it at half the height at time 0.75 and at the tip at 1, a `random` with `once` moves it up or down by a tenth of the stem, and about 40 % of the clusters in the band get 1–3 pedicels with a flower each: five broad rounded petals in one whorl (`face` path), golden at their base and paler at their edge. The template opens at time 0.88, with the band in the upper half of the spike.

**Growth:** the parts come one after another. The rosette grows leaf by leaf from the outside in until time 0.5 (`time × 4 − alpha` into the curve of the leaf size). The stem bolts from 0.2 to 0.6, and its `growth` rises on to 1.5, so `path_info` age tells every leaf, wing and cluster how long ago the tip passed it: a leaf and its wing grow behind the tip, a cluster of the spike swells after it, the lowest first, and its buds and bracts swell with it (`path_info` grown of the cluster). The flowers open from 0.7 on.

**Per seed:** the height, the length of the spike, how thick it is, the size of the leaves, the number of rosette and stem leaves, the lean and where the band of flowers sits. A short plant with a short spike over a wide rosette and a tall one with a spike of half its height both come up.

**Colours:** grey-green leaves with pale veins, a grey-green stem, buds and bracts, yellow flowers.

Missing: the hairs as geometry, the taproot, seed capsules and the dry brown spike of autumn. A plant has about one million triangles, most of them in the buds of the spike.

## Ribwort Plantain: _Plantago lanceolata_ (ribwort plantain, narrowleaf plantain)

Family Plantaginaceae.

**Parts:** short crown with fibrous roots, a basal rosette of leaves, leafless grooved scapes, and a dense spike of small flowers on each, whose long white stamens hang out in a ring.

**Structure:** A perennial rosette 10–45 cm tall. 8–25 leaves, lanceolate, 5–20 cm long and 1–3 cm wide, with 3–5 prominent parallel veins, an acute tip and a base that narrows into a reddish petiole. They stand up from the crown at an angle. The scapes are 1.5–3 times as long as the leaves, 1–2 mm thick. The spike is 1–3 cm long, ovoid to cylindrical. It flowers from the base up: below the ring of anthers the spike is brown, above it the buds are dark green.

**Template:** one `random` with `once` (the habit) sets the number of leaves (9–24) and of scapes (4–14) and the length of the scapes, more of them shorter. The leaves sit on the points of a `spiral` that rises 4 mm, at about the golden angle (2.4 rad), 0.35 rad above flat for the outer ones to 1.25 rad for the inner ones, ± 0.25 rad per leaf, and the inner ones are the longest. A leaf is a `leaf` of one of four `shape` outlines, 6–8 times as long as wide: a long wedge into a narrow winged base, widest a little above the middle and a long drawn-out tip. `x_resolution 13` gives it columns of vertices along its length, so a `set_color` with `alpha` across into a curve draws dark parallel veins on the midrib and two on each side. Three `deform` nodes then fold it into a shallow V (curl 0.35), twist it by a `random` of ±0.7 rad and bend it outward and down by a `random` of −2 to −0.8 rad, more toward the tip, so the longer leaves arch over; the four outlines get four different twists and bends. 7–13 thin pale roots leave the crown 2.2–2.8 rad from upright, spread around it (`rotate y` with `spread`), wander a little by `noise` and bend down by `gravity`. They grow out first, each by the `growth` of its `stem`, `time × 2.2 − random per root 0–0.35`. The leaves come one after another, from the outside in: each grows to its size by `curve(time × 2.1 − 0.6 × alpha − random per leaf 0–0.35)`.

The scapes are a `stem` of 10–40 cm, leaned up to 0.35 rad and spread around the plant (`rotate y` with `spread`), with a little `noise` and `gravity`. Every scape opens at its own time: `phase = time × 1.6 − random per path 0–0.45` drives the `growth` of the scape, so it grows up from its base at its full length instead of stretching, then the spike, a `branch` of 1.4–3.2 cm at its tip: it appears whole as a small bud when the scape is up and swells to its size (its length and the size of its bracts follow a curve of the phase), and last `opening`, another curve of the same phase. On the spike 90 bracts (`instance`, `face` path) pick a green or a brown bract by their index, `(1.7 − 2.6 × alpha) × opening`, so the brown part rises from the base as the spike opens. The same value draws the ring of stamens where the green turns brown: 110 `branch` filaments on the spike, as long as the ring is wide there and drooping by `gravity`, each with a tiny white anther at its tip, a `revolve` that an `instance` with `lowest_instance 1` places along the filament and sizes by its `path_info` length.

**Colours:** mid-green leaves with dark green veins, the scapes reddish at the base, the spike dark green above and brown below, the filaments and anthers white.

Missing: the grooves of the scape, the hairs, the seed capsules.
