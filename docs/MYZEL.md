# Myzel

Myzel is the node system in `nodes/max/myzel`. Its nodes grow mushrooms. They work on the paths of [Plantarium](./PLANTARIUM.md): a stipe is a `stem`, the cap is rays that grow on it, strands fill both, and `hyphae_to_mesh` turns the strands into one body of tissue. The shared code is the crate `myzel_core` in `packages/myzel/src`, its module docs describe the meshes and what paths carry for them.

Myzel nodes pass `path`, `points`, `pattern`, `geometry` and `color` between them. A link carries exactly its type, there are no hidden items in a list of paths. Their surfaces are geometry like the ones of the plant nodes (see Geometry in [PLANTARIUM.md](./PLANTARIUM.md)), with a material group per vertex and the node that made every group: Plantarium's `join` joins them, its `set_color` paints them, and the plant nodes that take geometry take them too. Before graph version 15 they were a type of their own, `mesh`, that `join_meshes` joined and `set_mesh_color` painted, the migration of the graph replaces them.

## The workflow

```
       time, or one age per mushroom, into every group

  Stipe ──stipe──▶ Cap ───rays───▶ Gills
   │ │              │ │              │
   │ │              │ └─margin─┐     │
   │ └─────stipe────┼───────▶ Veil   │
   │                │          │     │
 strands         strands      geom  geom
   │                │          │     │
   └─────▶ join ◀───┘          ▼     ▼
            │                  join ──▶ to_geometry ──▶ output
            ▼                   ▲
     hyphae_to_mesh ───geometry─┘
```

Every mushroom template is built from the same groups:

- **Stipe**: a `stem`, posed by Plantarium nodes, filled with `strands`. Gives the stipe as a path and its strands, colored.
- **Cap**: a `cap` at a point at the top of the stipe (`points_on_path` with one point at 1), posed by `fill_gaps`, `margin_waves`, `margin_curl` and `cap_open`, filled with its own `strands`. Gives the rays (with the stipe they grow on) and their strands, colored.
- **Gills**: a `reaction_diffusion` pattern under the rays, made into plates by `lamellae` or folds by `ridges`. Gives geometry.
- **Pores** (instead of the Gills where the species has tubes): a net of `cracks` under the rays, made into a sponge of tubes by `pores`. Gives geometry.
- **Veil** (only where the species has one): a `membrane` from the cap margin to a point on the stipe, and `skirt`s for the parts it tears into. Gives geometry.
- **Cuticle** (only where the cap has a pattern): `cap_pattern` puts patterns from `cracks` or `reaction_diffusion` on the top of the cap of the body from `hyphae_to_mesh`, before the `join`. Gives the body.
- **Reticulation** (only where the stipe has a net): `cap_pattern` puts `cracks` around the stipe alone, on the body from `hyphae_to_mesh`, before the `join`. Gives the body.
- **Volva** (only where the stipe grows from a bulb with remnants of the universal veil): `skirt`s around points on the top of the bulb, the ragged rims. Gives geometry.
- **Ring** (only where the veil leaves no more than a ring low on the stipe): a `skirt` around a point on every stipe. Gives geometry.
- **Substrate** (what the mushrooms grow on, not part of the templates, `withSubstrate()` of `@nodarium/runtime/templates` adds it): a `mesh` of a stump, moss or litter and the places on it from `sample_mesh`. Gives the substrate as geometry for the `join` and the places as points for the `stem` in the Stipe group.

A group takes outcome inputs, like lengths, radii, colors and the time or the age, and gives paths or geometry. At the top level the strands of stipe and cap are joined and go through `hyphae_to_mesh`, a second `join` joins that body with the gills and the veil into one geometry, the groups of each numbered after those of the ones before it, and the `output` of [Core](./CORE.md) shows it, through a `to_geometry` that passes it on as it is.

## Nodes

Paths:

- `cap`: stipes (`plant`) and `points` → the stipes and the rays of a cap at every point. `radius`, `profile`, flesh `thickness`, number of `rays`.
- `fill_gaps` (Plantarium): rays → the rays and new ones where neighbours drift apart toward the margin.
- `margin_waves`: rays → rays whose margin waves up and down in lobes. `waves`, `amplitude`, `uneven_spacing`.
- `margin_curl`: rays → rays whose margin rolls in under the cap. `amount`.
- `cap_open`: rays → the cap closed to a button (0) or open as drawn (1). `opening`.
- `strands`: guide paths → a bundle of strands in the tube of every guide. `amount`, `thickness`, `wobble`.
- `set_fibres`, `set_fill`: paths → the same paths with a fibre relief or a fill for `hyphae_to_mesh` at every point, see Fibres and fill. The color of the strands comes from Plantarium's `set_color`, see Colors.

Points:

- `cap_margin`: rays → the margin of every cap as a ring of points, for `membrane` and `skirt`.

Pattern:

- `reaction_diffusion`: → stripes on a disc, every stripe a gill. `radius`, `inner_radius`, `spacing`, `alignment`, `irregularity`, `forking`.
- `cracks`: → the cracks of a cuticle torn into patches on a disc, 1 in a crack. `radius`, `spacing`, `width`, `stretch`, `softness`. Narrow cracks are a cracked old cap, wide ones leave scales, patches stretched along the radius are fibrils.

Geometry:

- `hyphae_to_mesh`: paths → one body of tissue around them, colored by the colors of their points. `fibres`, `fill`, the `resolution.field` setting.
- `lamellae`: rays and a pattern → thin plates with a sharp edge, free of the stipe or running down it. `depth`, `free_distance`, `decurrent`, `thickness`, `edge_wave`.
- `ridges`: rays and a pattern → low blunt folds, never deeper than wide, that run down the stipe. `depth`, `decurrent`, `thickness`, `edge_wave`.
- `pores`: rays and a pattern → a layer of tubes under the cap that open in pores where the pattern is 0, its walls where it is 1. `depth`, `free_distance`, `pore_depth`.
- `membrane`: two attachments `a` and `b` (points or paths) → the closed skin between them. `thickness`, `sag`.
- `skirt`: one attachment → a skin that hangs from it to a torn edge. `length`, `flare`, `thickness`. Only points at the tips of their paths, like a margin, line up into a ring, so a point on every stipe of a cluster hangs a skin around each.
- `cap_pattern`: geometry, the rays of a cap and a pattern → the geometry with the pattern on the top of the cap, as a relief and a color. A path in the list that bears no cap, like the stipe alone, gets it around its side. `relief`, `slide`, `color`, `opacity`.

Substrates:

- `mesh`: → a substrate in real size, baked into the node: `stump` (33 cm across on a mound of forest floor 56 cm across), `moss` (a cushion 23 cm across), `needles` and `leaves` (litter 29 cm across), each with the colors of its scan or, with `colored` off, without them. See `nodes/max/myzel/mesh/bake/SOURCES.md`.
- `sample_mesh`: geometry → points on its surface, pointing along its normal. `amount`, `spacing`, `facing_up`.

The mushrooms end in the one `output` of [Core](./CORE.md), which takes only geometry. A debug node shows paths as lines and a pattern as a disc. Up to graph version 17 Myzel had an output of its own that did both.

The Plantarium nodes the templates use besides `stem`: `trunk_kink`, `rotate` and `keep_apart` pose and space the stipes, `points_on_path` places the cap and the veil, `separate` takes the rays apart from the stipe so the cap gets its own `strands`, `twist`, `random_break`, `clump` and `neighbour_sensing` give the strands their grown look, and `path_info` reads the stipe. The values come from the nodes of [Core](./CORE.md): `time` and `mix_color` (Myzel nodes up to graph version 15), `curve`, `random`, `math`, `round`, `random_int` and `vec3`.

## Conventions

These came out of the milestone "Myzel cleanup" (#212 to #224).

**One job per node.** A node does one thing and has at most 8 inputs. What another node can do moves out: the `twist` and the length variation of `strands` became the Plantarium `twist` and `random_break`, the lopsided cap became a `curve` into the cap radius, the torn parts of the veil became `skirt`, and the color of the strands comes from Plantarium's `set_color` like the color of any path. `hyphae_to_mesh` meshes paths, `join` joins geometry, the `output` only shows.

**Shape with `curve`, `random` and `alpha`.** A node has no inputs that shape another of its inputs (no `reach`, `irregularity`, `depth_curve` or `flare` curve). A `curve` into the input draws the profile, a `random` varies it, `alpha` (or a `curve` without a value) reads where the node is. Where a `curve` would only scale a `random`, the `random` goes into the `min` or `max` of the curve, so no `math` node is needed: how far the margin of a chanterelle waves is a `curve` of its age up to a `random` per mushroom between 0.07 and 0.17.

**What is read where.** A node reads an input once for everything it stands for, and sets alpha and the path first, so a `random` or a `curve` varies it there:

**One age per mushroom.** A `random` with `per_path` draws one value for every path, the same in every node that reads it. The chanterelle draws one age per stipe at the top level and gives it to every group, so its stipe, the size of its cap, how far the cap opens and how much its margin curls and waves all follow the same age. The Champignon has one specimen and reads `time` instead.

**One specimen per seed.** Every seed grows another specimen of the same species. The templates draw what makes it at the top level and give it to the groups as their inputs, within the range of the species:

- A `random` with `once` ("once for the graph") is one value for the whole graph, read the same by the nodes on the stipe and by the nodes without a path, like `reaction_diffusion` under the cap or `cracks` on it. The single mushrooms draw a size this way and multiply it into the length and the radius of the stipe and the radius of the cap, each drawn again on its own, so the proportions vary too. A `curve` of alpha with a `random` into its `max` makes the cap reach further on one side, and the reach of the gills and the radius of the pattern on the cap follow the radius, as they did before.
- A `mix_color` with a `random` factor between two shades of the species colors the cap: a white to tan Champignon, a deep red to orange Fly agaric, a light to dark Porcini. A troop or a cluster takes one shade for all its mushrooms.
- Inside the groups, a `random` into the `max` of the curve of time into `cap_open.opening` leaves some caps domed and opens others flat, one into the `min` of the curve into `margin_curl.amount` keeps the margin rolled in on some. A `random` per kink into `trunk_kink` `angle` and `rotation` and a `rotate` around the axis lean and bend every stipe to another side.
- The spacing of gills, pores, scales, warts and cracks, the depth of the gills and the height of the ring are `random`s into the inputs of their groups.
- A troop or a cluster draws the spread of its ages (`random` once into the `min` and `max` of the age per path), the number of stipes (`random_int`) and, with a wider `random` around the place of every stipe, where they stand.

**The cap grows at points.** `cap` takes the paths and the points to grow at, usually `points_on_path` with `amount` 1 and `lowest_point` = `highest_point` = 1 on the stipe. Its output is the stipes plus the rays, like `branch_from_points`. The pose nodes, `lamellae` and `ridges` take this one list and find the frame of every cap on the stipe the rays grow on (its place, axis and radius at the point). They bend only rays a `cap` made, every other path passes through, so the nodes can be chained in any order.

**`lamellae` or `ridges`.** True gills are `lamellae`: thin plates with a sharp edge, free of the stipe (`free_distance`, Agaricus) or running down it (`decurrent`, the oyster). False gills are `ridges`: low folds with a round back that run down the stipe, like the chanterelle. Both trace the stripes of a `reaction_diffusion` pattern.

**`membrane` and `skirt`.** `membrane` is the closed skin between two attachments, for example the margin from `cap_margin` and a point on the stipe from `points_on_path`. `skirt` hangs a skin from one attachment: around a point on a path it hangs out all around like a ring, from a ring of points it hangs on the inside like the fringe on a margin. On a ring its strands are spaced evenly along the points, so it follows a lopsided or wavy margin, and start inside the round end of the paths the points sit on (a ray of the cap, as thick as the point's scale), so the skin grows out of the flesh. A ring reads `length` and `flare` at every point: a `random` into them tears the fringe into flaps of different length and hang, a length of 0 leaves a gap. The Champignon switches from the membrane to two skirts when the veil tears, with curves of time into their thickness and length; its fringe gets a `random` length per point whose share of gaps grows with time, and a `random` into the `max` of its flare curve.

**Fill.** `hyphae_to_mesh` fills the gaps between strands a ball of `fill` radii of the thickest strand can't pass. Every ray of a cap carries the same number of strands however thick its flesh is, so the strands lie furthest apart where the flesh is thickest, around the middle of a large cap. A `set_fill` of 4 on the strands of the Cap closes the flesh there, while the stipe keeps the lower fill of `hyphae_to_mesh` and shows its strands. Without it the old caps of the Chanterelle had holes around the centre of their top. The blur that smooths the filled tissue wears away a tissue thinner than about the fill, and a strand whose tube sticks out of what is left keeps its tube, so a lone strand keeps its radius at any fill.

**Fibres and fill.** `set_fibres` and `set_fill` write their value into every point of the paths that pass through them, like `set_color` its color (a path carries them after its points, its type says which, see `point_values` in `nodarium_utils::geometry`). `hyphae_to_mesh` meshes a segment of a path with the mean of its two points, the paths without them with its own `fibres` and `fill`. The value is read for every path and along it, so a `random` per path or a curve of `alpha` shapes it, a strand that loses its relief towards the tip for example. A node that makes a path anew (`cut`, `random_break`, `trunk_kink`) keeps the values where they were along it, like the colors. Up to `@nodarium/runtime` 1.2 the two nodes put their value next to the paths as a hidden item for every path of the nodes that made them, the graphs stay as they were.

**Colors.** Plantarium's `set_color` colors the strands and the geometry. On strands it gives every point a color, and `hyphae_to_mesh` colors the surface around them with it. Where the strands of two colors meet, the color goes over smoothly. The strands of a node that has one color everywhere share it with every other strand of that color. A node whose strands change color, along a strand by a curve of `alpha` or from strand to strand by a `random` per path, gets the color of its nearest strand at every vertex, at the point of the strand nearest to the vertex. So a stipe can go from a dark base to a pale top. The Stipe and Cap groups color their strands with a `depth` of 10, so all levels get the color. Up to graph version 18, Myzel had a `set_color` of its own that put the color next to the paths as a hidden item. The migration replaces it with this one. On geometry `set_color` keeps the groups. A `mix_color` with a curve of time or age as its factor lets a color follow it, like the gills of the Champignon that darken.

**Patterns on the cap.** A pattern is a disc around the axis of the cap, its radius the distance along the rays, so `lamellae`, `ridges` and `cap_pattern` find a spot of it on the same spot of the tissue however the cap is posed. `cap_pattern` puts it on the top of the cap only: on the side of the flesh that faces away from the gills, in the material group most of that top is in, so a stipe, gills or a veil joined into the mesh stay as they are. It splits the faces there until they are as fine as the pattern. The Fly agaric turns it round: the flesh of its cap is white like the universal veil over it, and its `cracks` show the red cuticle under the veil, pressed in so the patches between them stand up as warts. One disc covers the largest cap of the troop, its spacing a share of that radius, and a curve of alpha along the disc opens the cracks towards its edge: a small young cap only reaches the middle of the disc, where the warts lie close together, an open one also its edge, where they stand apart on the red. The `opacity` is a curve of the age of every mushroom, so the egg stays white and the red shows through as the cap swells, and the warts flatten with the age.

The Shiitake puts two on its cap, both with the white of its flesh. Raised `cracks` stretched along the radius are its white fibrous scales. The pattern is one disc for all caps, as large as the largest can be, so `opacity` and `relief` keep the scales to the margin of every cap whatever its size: a curve of alpha along the rays, up to a curve of the age of the mushroom, which wears them off as its cap grows. Its `flower` input opens round `cracks` all over the cap and presses them in, the dry "flower" shiitake (hua gu) whose cracks show the white flesh: 0 is a smooth cap, about 0.25 a flower shiitake. Both have their spacing as a share of the radius of the largest cap, and the flower cracks their width as a share of the spacing.

The Shaggy inkcap puts three on its cap, in this order. A pattern that is 1 everywhere colors the smooth top of the cap brown with a curve of alpha, and black with the ink: its opacity is a curve of half the alpha plus a curve of the age, so the ink starts at the margin of a cap that ripens and climbs up it. Then `cracks` stretched around the axis are its shaggy scales: pressed in and slid towards the axis with a negative `slide`, so the flesh between the scales tucks in under them and the lower edge of every scale lifts off the cap. A relief alone only moves the surface in or out, the slide makes the overhang. The scales come after the ink, so the flesh that tucks in keeps its ink, and they color the flesh between them white only where there is no ink yet.

**Pores.** `pores` fills the underside of a cap with a layer of tubes, like `lamellae` it reads the rays and finds a spot of its pattern on the same spot of the tissue however the cap is posed. The walls of the tubes are where the pattern is 1, so the cells of `cracks` are the pores and its cracks their walls. The tubes hang from the underside between straight down from it and straight down the axis, and are shorter where they would come closer to the stipe than `free_distance`, so on a closed button they are pressed into the space between cap and stipe, and on the shut button there is none. The Porcini makes the tubes longer with a curve of time and shapes the layer along the cap with a curve of alpha into `depth`, which is the outline of the cushion seen from the side: short at the stipe, so the layer sinks in around it (with a `free_distance` of 4 mm), longest from 0.4 to 0.7, and a quarter circle down to 0 at the margin, so the layer has a round shoulder instead of a wall. A tube points halfway between the normal of the underside and the axis, so under a margin that turns down steeply the tubes lean in and the layer becomes a cone with a sharp rim. The profile of the Porcini cap therefore flattens towards the margin (about 27° there), and thicker flesh over the stipe keeps the cap domed. It opens the pores with a curve of time into `pore_depth` and colors them with a `mix_color` of time from white to yellow or olive.

**A net on the stipe.** `cap_pattern` puts its pattern around a path of its list that bears no cap and grows on no other path of it, like the stipe out of the Stipe group: the middle of the pattern on the tip of the path, its radius the distance from there over the surface, across the end and down the side, so the side only takes the outer part of the disc and the pattern is stretched along the path the further down it is. The relief and the opacity are read along the path, alpha 0 at its base to 1 at its tip. The Porcini lays `cracks` stretched along the radius around its stipe, raised and white, with a curve of alpha that keeps the net on the upper part.

**Substrates.** The substrate comes from the `mesh` node, not grown with nodes: a scan from Poly Haven (CC0), cut down to a few thousand triangles, with its colors in its vertices. `sample_mesh` spreads the places over it where the stipes start: a stipe of the `stem` node starts on a point and grows along its direction, out of the wood or the ground, and the stipes take the places in turn. A troop has as many places as stipes, a cluster a few, so the stipes of a place grow out of one spot like a tuft. A place only depends on the seed and the places before it, more places keep the ones there are. `facing_up` keeps them on level ground, the top of a stump or the floor around it, so the stipes stand upright. `keep_apart` with `lean` turns a stipe around its base instead of moving it, so it stays on its place. The Substrate group goes into the last input of `join`, its group comes after those of the mushroom, so the groups of the mushroom keep their numbers. Its `colored` input switches the colors of the scan off, the viewer then shows the substrate in its material. Its geometry names the `mesh` node as the creator of its one group, so hovering the substrate points out that node. The templates come without a substrate, their stipes stand where the template puts them. `withSubstrate()` of `@nodarium/runtime/templates` adds the Substrate group to a mushroom graph, `substrates` keeps the one each of `porcini`, `chanterelle`, `honey-fungus`, `sulphur-tuft`, `false-chanterelle` and `bitter-bolete` grew on before, for the Pilz-Tagebuch.

**Groups.** A template groups its nodes into Stipe, Cap, Gills and Veil as above, so the top level reads as the workflow. Group inputs are the outcomes someone tunes, everything else stays inside.
